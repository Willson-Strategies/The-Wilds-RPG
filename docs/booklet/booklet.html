How to play

Starting the Game

Four strangers, one castle, and the wilds beyond

Create a party of four adventurers to go and find allies within the strange new land you find yourselves in. The last castle stands at the edge of the wilds. Beyond its walls lie rat-choked roads, haunted woods and worse, and everything out there would happily see you dead.

Raise your party

  1. Choose a quest slot. You can keep up to five quests going at once, each with its own party. Every quest saves itself as you play and carries on exactly where you left it. There is no going back, so choose with care.
  2. Pick four heroes. Any mix of the six classes, and the same class more than once if you like. Give each hero a name.
  3. Fate rolls their stats. Each hero's stats are rolled with their class's dice, and there are no rerolls.
  4. Enter the castle. The party sets out with a purse of 5 GP or more for each hero.

How to play

Playing the Game

Out into the wilds, and back again

Travel through the wilds fighting monsters and gaining treasures, then return to the castle to equip yourselves with stronger weapons and spells from its shops. Each area of the wilds is tougher than the last, and its monsters guard better rewards.

In the castle

The Inn
A safe night behind the walls. Everyone wakes at full health with every spell restored, level 2 spells included.
Weapon & Armour Shop
Better steel and heavier armour. Finer wares appear as your party grows in fame.
Magic Shop
Scrolls that teach new spells to your casters.
Blacksmith
Polishes worn weapons and armour back to their best.
The Clerics
Heal the party, cleanse ailments and raise the fallen, free of charge.
The Caravan
Takes you straight to any place you have already visited.
Party & gear
Equip weapons and armour, fill spellbooks and set the marching order.

In the wilds

Explore
Time moves on until something finds you: monsters, a scrap of lore, a hidden cache or a chest.
Make camp
Rest for as many hours as you choose. Each hour heals every living hero D3 HP (D3+1 with a living Cleric). 8 hours restores level 1 spells; 16 hours restores level 2 spells too. The longer you rest, the more likely something finds you, and an ambush always strikes first.
Return to the castle
Head home to heal, shop and sleep. The road back is not always quiet.

Monsters approach

When monsters find you, you see who is coming and choose to Fight or Flee. A party that flees wins no experience or treasure, and if the escape fails the monsters strike first.

In battle

On each hero's turn you can Attack with their melee weapon, Shoot with a sling or bow, Cast a spell from their spellbook, Wait, or try to Flee. Hover over an enemy to see the Roll needed to hit it, the damage you would deal and the chance of any ailment. The Combat pages explain every roll.

Marching order

The first two heroes in the party stand at the front and take most of the blows: monsters attack them about three times as often as the two at the back. Drag the hero cards in Party & gear to change the order.

When heroes fall

A hero at 0 HP falls and takes no further part in the battle. If the rest of the party wins, the fallen hero earns only a tenth of their experience, and stays down until the Clerics raise them.

If the whole party falls, a passing caravan carries them back to the castle. Thieves take half your gold on the way, and your gear takes a beating.

Growing stronger

Every monster defeated earns experience, and heroes level up all the way to level 10, rolling new stats and HP each time. Spend your gold on better weapons, armour and scrolls, and push on into deeper, deadlier places. Once your party has outgrown an area, monsters there find you less often.

Armour Class (AC)

AC is how hard a hero is to hit. An attacker rolls a D20 and adds their to-hit bonus; if the total equals or beats your AC, the blow lands. The higher the armour class, the harder your character is to hit.

The six stats

Strength The weight behind every blow. The wilds respect only what they cannot push aside.

Adds to the damage of every weapon blow, melee or ranged.

Dexterity Quick hands and quicker feet decide whether a blade glances off or finds its mark.

Adds to every weapon attack roll, and to AC.

Constitution The grit to keep breathing long after the road has bled you dry.

Adds bonus HP upon level up.

Wisdom An old, quiet knowing that steadies a healer's hand over torn flesh.

Adds to every healing spell. The Elf and Thief add half.

Intelligence A trained and hungry mind that bends raw power into fire and lightning.

Adds to every damage spell (the Elf and Thief add half, and Fireball half again), and each point adds 1% to the chance of Sleep and Entangle.

Charisma A steady gaze and a silver tongue, worth more than coin in a hungry land.

Adds up to 5 GP to the starting purse. In time it will also sway shop prices and encounters.

Weapons, spells and monsters can leave a target suffering long after the blow. An ailment only takes hold when the attack that carries it hits.

Wear and repair steel rusts, leather splits, and the wilds take their toll

Weapons, armour and shields wear while you play in the wilds and caves: a little for every real minute spent exploring or fighting there. Nothing wears in the castle or in camp, or while the game is not on screen. Wear shows on each item's picture as rust, chips and dried blood (updated when you make camp), and hovering over any item shows its durability and any penalty.

The rules of battle

Combat

Steel, spells and the roll of the dice

Who goes first

Initiative is rolled once, as battle begins, and holds for the whole fight. Heroes line up highest level first, and so do monsters (equal levels in a random order). The two lines are then merged: the next hero goes before the next monster with the chance below.

For example

A Fighter with Strength 16 swings a Sword: D3+1 from the blade and +3 for Strength, so D3+4, or 5 to 7 damage. On a critical hit, a roll of 7 becomes 10.

Spells

Damage spells roll their own dice and add the caster's Intelligence bonus; healing spells add the Wisdom bonus. The Elf and Thief add half the bonus. Fireball adds half again, then splashes half its damage onto every other enemy.

Monsters

Each monster rolls its own damage dice. Monsters never land critical hits: a natural 20 always hits them home, but for normal damage.

Critical hits

A natural 20 on the to-hit roll always hits and deals 50% more damage, rounded down, and at least 2. A Thief crits on any natural 15 or higher. A few rare weapons crit on a wider range. Monsters never crit.

20

Double Strike

Before each melee attack, a separate D20 is rolled. On a natural 20 the hero strikes twice, and each blow rolls to hit on its own. A Thief strikes twice on 15 or higher. Not for ranged attacks, spells or monsters, although some monsters attack twice every turn.

Dodges and damage reduction

Some blows can be dodged. The Thief evades 5% of non-magical hits at level 1, rising 1% a level to 14%. The Dwarf's stone skin has a 3% chance of reducing incoming damage by 1 from non-magical hits, rising to 12%. A few monsters slip blows too, and rare blades can parry.

Gold and treasure the spoils of a hard-won fight

Every monster you defeat may leave nothing, a purse of gold, or an item: a weapon, a piece of armour or a scroll. The stronger the monster, the more gold it carries and the finer its gear, and the mightiest can drop two lots of treasure.

Rarest of all are the unique weapons: named blades, each made for one class, that drop at most once in a whole quest. Anything you no longer need sells back to any shop for 15% of its price.

Fleeing a battle wins no treasure. A party wipe costs half your gold.

Fleeing

Any hero can try to flee on their turn, using that turn. The chance rests on how quick the party is against how strong the monsters still standing are, so a nimble party slips away more easily. If the attempt fails, the turn is lost. A fleeing party wins no experience or treasure, and some battles cannot be fled at all.

Ambushes

Monsters that catch the party resting, or that cut off a failed escape before battle, strike first: every one of them acts before the party in the first round. Ambushed heroes find it far harder to get away. Rest somewhere safe, like the Inn, when you can.

Play style
Simple and direct

The Fighter is a professional warrior, trained with every weapon in the armoury. Where others rely on spells or tricks, the Fighter trusts steel and a thick hide, standing at the front to take the blows meant for their friends.

Strengths

  • Dexterity tops out at 20, so armour does most of the defending

Using your spellbook

  • Learning. You can learn every Magic User spell. Buy scrolls in the shop or find them as loot; there is no limit to how many you know.
  • Slots. Only spells written into your spellbook slots can be cast. Choose which spells fill your slots to suit the fight ahead.
  • Casting. Each filled slot gives one cast. Once cast, that slot is spent until the party rests.
  • Doubles. The same spell may fill more than one slot. You begin with Magic Missile in both of your first two slots.
  • Level 2 spells. Only slots marked ✦ may hold a level 2 spell.
  • Always hits. Spells never miss. Damage spells add your Intelligence bonus.

Using your spellbook

  • Learning. You can learn every Cleric spell. Buy scrolls in the shop or find them as loot; there is no limit to how many you know.
  • Slots. Only spells written into your spellbook slots can be cast. Choose which prayers fill your slots to suit the fight ahead.
  • Casting. Each filled slot gives one cast. Once cast, that slot is spent until the party rests.
  • Doubles. The same spell may fill more than one slot. You begin with Undead Ward and Cure Light Wounds.
  • Level 2 spells. Only slots marked ✦ may hold a level 2 spell.
  • Always lands. Spells never miss. Healing adds your Wisdom bonus; spells against the undead add your Intelligence bonus.

The city

Within the Castle Walls

Steel, leather and a warm hearth, for a price

The castle still has plenty of stock of basic weapons and armour, and its smiths and armourers will craft finer wares given time. Some weapons, armour and spells cannot be bought at all: they lie out in the wilderness, waiting to be found. Be sure to stock up before heading out.

The Clerics

The temple's clerics tend to every pilgrim who returns from the wilds. Your party is restored to full health, ailments are cleansed and fallen companions are raised, all free of charge. Raising the fallen takes the night: the whole party rests at the temple and wakes with every spell restored.

The Caravan

Merchant caravans leave the castle gates every day. Take one to any place you have visited before and skip the long walk back through the wilderness.

Selling

Every shop will buy back anything you no longer need for 15% of its price. Better a few coins than dead weight in the pack.
